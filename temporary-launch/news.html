DIGITAL PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE

Why UNDP’s work on DPI matters to us.

Digital public infrastructure can create shared digital foundations for services such as identity, payments and data exchange. When these systems are inclusive, interoperable and protected by strong safeguards, they can make it easier for people to access public services and participate in the digital economy.

UNDP has connected DPI to progress across all 17 Sustainable Development Goals. This matters to NEURORDER because our own work explores how digital identity, responsible payments and alternative credit systems may support people who are often excluded from formal economic systems.

THE 2030 AGENDA

A future built around people, planet and shared prosperity.

The United Nations 2030 Agenda is a global plan of action centred on people, planet and prosperity. It includes 17 Sustainable Development Goals that call for progress in areas such as poverty, education, equality, innovation, infrastructure, decent work and strong institutions.

For us, the Agenda is not simply a list of global targets. It is a reminder that technology should produce measurable human value. Systems should be judged by who they include, what barriers they remove and whether they help communities build better futures.

DID YOU KNOW?

Questions worth carrying with you.

Did you know that digital identity can influence access to banking, education and public services?

When designed responsibly, trusted digital identity can help people prove who they are and access services more efficiently. Poorly designed systems, however, can create new forms of exclusion.

Did you know that digital infrastructure is not only a technology issue?

Governance, privacy, accessibility, public trust and legal safeguards are just as important as the software itself.

Did you know that one headline can be accurate while still missing important context?

Facts need interpretation, history and comparison. Nous News aims to help readers understand the larger system surrounding a story.

Did you know that young people are already shaping the future of computing?

Students, researchers and young founders are contributing to artificial intelligence, public technology, fintech and scientific discovery across Africa and the world.
